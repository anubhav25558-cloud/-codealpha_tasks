## 1. Automation System Design — Smart Home Lighting System

**File:** `Task3_Automation_System_Design_Report.docx`

An engineering design report for a smart home lighting system that combines a PIR motion sensor and an LDR (light sensor) to automatically switch lights on when a room is occupied and dark, and off again after a period of no motion.

**Covers:**
- Objective and system components (PIR sensor, LDR, microcontroller, relay module, LED, software timer)
- Working principle, with the full decision logic as a flowchart
- Advantages (energy saving, convenience, extendability, low cost)
- Applications (homes, offices, corridors, parking areas, street lighting)

**Read it as:** a hardware/embedded systems design document — useful as a reference for the sensor logic and control flow behind the project.

## 2. Sector Briefing — Robots on the Battlefield, Farm, and Doorstep

**File:** `SERVICE_ROBOTS_REPORT.pdf`

A July 2026 sector briefing surveying service robotics — machines that perform useful tasks outside factory manufacturing — across three domains: military/defence, agriculture, and domestic/eldercare.

**Covers:**
- Comparative analysis of the three domains (goals, environments, sensors, autonomy level, adoption barriers)
- Military unmanned systems (UGVs, UAVs, EOD robots, maritime systems, exoskeletons) and the ethics/regulation of autonomous force
- Agricultural robotics (autonomous tractors, robotic weeders, fruit-picking robots, crop-monitoring drones) and adoption barriers for smallholder farms
- Domestic service robots (robot vacuums, lawn mowers, eldercare/companion robots) and the privacy questions raised by continuous home sensing
- Shared economic dynamics, including the shift toward Robotics-as-a-Service (RaaS)
- Outlook: convergence of enabling technology (sensing, AI/ML, edge computing, batteries) across all three domains

**Read it as:** a research/analysis briefing — useful as background reading on where service robotics is headed and how the underlying technology overlaps with the automation project above.

## How These Relate

Both documents sit at different ends of the same theme: the lighting system report is a hands-on design exercise in sensor-driven automation at small scale, while the sector briefing places that same category of technology (sensing, autonomy, edge processing) in a much broader industry context — from home robots up through agriculture and defence.
